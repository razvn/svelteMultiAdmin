Audit log

The portal has no audit log of its own. The two apps already record who did what, and the portal gives them everything they need: the person's name, their role or account, and the real client address.

Everyone behind the portal shares one account per database (one Redis password, one MySQL user), so the database cannot tell them apart. Turn on AuditLog in each app's own configuration to record the person: 

{ "AuditLog": { "target": "stdout" } }

One JSON line per session, write, console line, export, import and refusal. These are real lines from a portal with both apps (an administrator opening each app): 

{"ts":"2026-11-12T10:27:23.674Z","event":"session","identity":"admin","role":"admin","server":"Dev redis1","serverId":"0","clientIp":"127.0.0.1"}
{"ts":"2026-11-12T10:27:23.982Z","event":"session","identity":"admin","mysqlUser":"root","server":"Local MySQL","serverId":"1","clientIp":"127.0.0.1"}
  • identity is the person's name as the portal knows it, whatever the sign-in mode.
  • role (Redis) is the role from the access rules. mysqlUser (MySQL) is the shared account the app connected with.
  • clientIp is the real client address. Behind an SSO proxy the portal resolves it from X-Forwarded-For through your trustedProxies, and passes it on; otherwise it is the browser's address.
  • A refused request (no access to a server, a forged header) is logged with a reason, without an identity when nobody was verified.

Options (a file instead of stdout, values, retention) and the full list of events are in the apps' guides: svelteRedisAdmin audit log and svelteMyAdmin audit log. In Kubernetes, kubectl -n tools logs deploy/multiadmin shows both apps, each line prefixed with its app name by the portal.
